Finance & Accounting

Chapter 1: Introduction to Accounting — Exam-Oriented Notes

1. What is Accounting?

Definition

Accounting is the process of identifying, recording, classifying, summarizing, and communicating financial transactions to help users make informed business decisions.

Simple Meaning

Accounting converts daily business activities (sales, purchases, expenses, investments, etc.) into useful financial information.

2. Purpose of Accounting

Accounting helps a business:

  • Record financial transactions.
  • Measure profit or loss.
  • Understand financial position.
  • Prepare financial statements.
  • Support business decisions.
  • Meet legal and tax requirements.
  • Communicate financial information to stakeholders.

Exam Definition

Accounting is the language of business because it records, measures, and communicates financial information.

3. Why Do We Need Accounting?

A. Language of Business

  • Every business transaction is recorded in a standard format.
  • Makes financial information understandable worldwide.
Example:
Instead of saying — "Business is doing well"
Accounting says — Revenue = ₹50,00,000, Profit = ₹8,00,000

B. Performance Tracking

Helps answer:

  • Are we making profit?
  • Are expenses increasing?
  • Is revenue growing?

Without accounting → Decisions are based on guesses.

C. Compliance & Trust

Accounting ensures businesses:

  • Follow tax laws.
  • Follow government regulations.
  • Maintain transparency.
  • Build investor confidence.

Main Question Accounting Answers

How is the business performing financially, and what is its current financial position?

4. Functions of Accounting

Accounting has four major functions.

1. Recording (Bookkeeping)

Definition: Recording all financial transactions in chronological order.

Examples: Sales, Purchases, Salary, Rent, Electricity bill

Example: Sold goods for ₹20,000 → Record immediately.

2. Classifying

Definition: Grouping similar transactions together.

Examples: Sales Account, Salary Account, Rent Account, Travel Expenses, Electricity Expenses

Purpose: Makes analysis easier.

3. Summarizing

Definition: Converting recorded data into meaningful reports.

Main Reports: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement

Purpose: Provides an overall picture of business performance.

4. Interpreting

Definition: Analyzing financial reports for decision-making.

Used to answer:

  • Should we expand?
  • Should we reduce costs?
  • Can we take a loan?
  • Should we invest?

Memory Trick — RCSI

R → Record  |  C → Classify  |  S → Summarize  |  I → Interpret

5. Accounting Process

Business Transaction → Identify → Record → Classify → Summarize → Interpret → Communicate

6. Types of Accounting

A. Financial Accounting

Purpose: Prepare financial statements for external users.

Produces: Income Statement, Balance Sheet, Cash Flow Statement

Focus: Overall financial performance.

Users: Investors, Banks, Government, Creditors.

B. Tax Accounting

Purpose: Calculate taxes according to government laws.

Examples: Income Tax, GST, Corporate Tax

Focus: Legal compliance.

C. Cost Accounting

Purpose: Control and reduce production costs.

Focus: Fixed Cost, Variable Cost, Direct Cost, Indirect Cost

Example: Cost of producing one mobile phone.

D. Management Accounting

Purpose: Help managers make business decisions.

Includes: Budgeting, Forecasting, Capital Budgeting, Financial Analysis, Investment Decisions

Focus: Internal decision making.

Memory Trick — FTCM

F → Financial  |  T → Tax  |  C → Cost  |  M → Management

7. Users of Accounting Information

Accounting information is used by two groups.

Internal Users

Work inside the company.

Examples: Owners, Managers, Employees, Finance Department

Purpose: Planning, Budgeting, Performance Review, Forecasting, Pricing Decisions

External Users

People outside the company.

Examples: Investors, Banks, Creditors, Government, Tax Authorities, Regulators, Customers

Purpose: Investment decisions, Loan approval, Tax collection, Business evaluation

Memory Trick

Internal → Runs the business  |  External → Evaluates the business

8. GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)

Definition

GAAP is a set of standard accounting rules used while preparing financial statements.

Purpose:

  • Maintain consistency.
  • Improve accuracy.
  • Increase comparability.
  • Ensure transparency.

9. Revenue Recognition Principle

Revenue is recognized when:

  • ✓ Goods/services are delivered.
  • ✓ Revenue is earned.
  • ✗ Not necessarily when cash is received.
Example:
October: Laptop delivered.
December: Customer pays.
Revenue is recorded in October.
Keyword: Earned → Record Revenue

10. Expense Recognition (Matching Principle)

Definition: Expenses should be recorded in the same accounting period as the revenue they helped generate.
Example:
January: Sales commission earned.
February: Commission paid.
Expense is recorded in January.
Keyword: Revenue and related expenses should match

11. Measurement (Cost) Principle

Definition: Assets should be recorded at their original purchase cost (Historical Cost).
Example:
Land purchased in 2010 = ₹1,00,000
Current Market Value = ₹10,00,000
Accounting Value = ₹1,00,000

Reason: Accounting records actual cost, not market value.
Keyword: Historical Cost
