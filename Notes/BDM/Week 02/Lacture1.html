# 📚 DEMAND & SUPPLY — Complete Exam Notes ### *BDM | Economics for Managers | IITM Quiz + End-Term Ready* --- ## 🗺️ CHAPTER MIND MAP (See the whole topic in 10 seconds) ``` MARKET | +---------------+----------------+ | | BASED ON STRUCTURE BASED ON COMPETITION | | Organized / Unorganized Perfect Competition (Many sellers) Monopoly (One seller) Oligopoly (Few sellers) | ============================ || || DEMAND SUPPLY (Buyer side) (Seller side) | | Willing + Able to BUY Willing + Able to SELL | | Law of Demand Law of Supply (Price↑ → Qty demanded↓) (Price↑ → Qty supplied↑) | | Demand Schedule (table) Supply Schedule (table) | | Demand Curve (↓ sloping) Supply Curve (↑ sloping) | | Individual Demand → Market Demand Individual Supply → Market Supply (sum of all buyers) (sum of all sellers) ``` > 💼 **Business Insight:** Every pricing decision a company makes — from Zomato's surge pricing to Amazon's flash sales — is a live experiment on the Demand and Supply curves you're about to learn. This isn't abstract theory; it's the logic behind every "Price Drop" notification you get. --- ## 1️⃣ MARKET ### 📖 Definition (exam-safe, memorize as-is) > A **market** is a place or platform where **buyers and sellers interact** to exchange goods or services at a given price and time. **Core formula to remember:** ``` MARKET = BUYERS + SELLERS + EXCHANGE ``` | Real-world form | Example | |---|---| | Physical | Local vegetable market, Sunday haat | | Virtual/Online | Amazon, Flipkart, Zomato | --- ### 🔀 Types of Market — By Structure | Type | Fixed Place/Time? | Example | |---|---|---| | **Organized** | ✅ Yes | Farmer's mandi, Stock exchange | | **Unorganized** | ❌ No | Roadside ice-cream vendor, hawkers | --- ### 🔀 Types of Market — By Competition ⭐⭐⭐ (HIGH PYQ WEIGHT) This is the **single most tested sub-topic** in this chapter. Learn this table cold. | Feature | Perfect Competition | Monopoly | Oligopoly | |---|---|---|---| | **No. of sellers** | Many | **One** | **Few** | | **No. of buyers** | Many | Many | Many | | **Product type** | Homogeneous (identical) | Unique, no substitute | Similar/differentiated | | **Price control** | No one controls price | Seller fully controls price | Few firms jointly influence price | | **Price determined by** | Market forces (D & S) | The single seller | Interdependent firm decisions | | **Real example** | Vegetable market | IRCTC (rail ticketing), Indian Railways | Airtel, Jio, Vodafone (telecom) | > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** > **Perfect → Many** (P for Plenty) > **Monopoly → One** (Mono = 1) > **Oligopoly → Few** (Oligo = "a few" in Greek) > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** If a question gives you a scenario ("only one company supplies electricity in a city"), it's testing whether you can **identify** the market type from the description — not just recall the definition. Practice spotting keywords: "only one" → Monopoly; "2–5 major players" → Oligopoly; "countless small identical sellers" → Perfect Competition. > ❌ **Common Mistake:** Students confuse Oligopoly with Monopoly because both involve "limited" sellers. Remember: Monopoly = **1**, Oligopoly = **2 or more but few**. If there's more than one seller, it can NEVER be a monopoly. --- ## 2️⃣ DEMAND ⭐⭐⭐ ### 📖 Definition (exam-safe) > Demand is the **quantity of a good or service that consumers are willing AND able to buy at a given price, during a given time period.** ### 🔑 The Two-Condition Test Demand is NOT just "wanting" something. It requires **both**: | Condition | Meaning | |---|---| | **1. Willingness** | The person genuinely wants to buy it | | **2. Ability** | The person has the money to pay for it | ``` WANT + MONEY = DEMAND (Willingness) (Ability) ✔ ✔ ✅ ✔ ❌ ❌ (this is only DESIRE) ``` > 💼 **Business Scenario:** You want a BMW (Willingness ✔) but your bank balance is ₹5,000 (Ability ❌). > → This is **NOT Demand**. It is only a **desire/wish**. > This distinction matters a lot to businesses — a company doesn't care how many people "want" its product; it cares how many people can **afford** it. This is why luxury brands target income brackets, not just interest groups. > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** **Demand = Want + Money.** No money → No demand, only desire. --- ### 📉 Law of Demand ⭐⭐⭐ > **Definition:** *Ceteris Paribus* (other things remaining constant), **when price increases, quantity demanded decreases** — and vice versa. ``` Price ↑ ──────────► Quantity Demanded ↓ Price ↓ ──────────► Quantity Demanded ↑ ``` **Why does this happen?** ``` Price rises → Purchasing power effectively falls → People buy less ``` | Price of Ice Cream | Quantity Bought | |---|---| | ₹20 | 5 | | ₹40 | 2 | > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** Think like a shopper at a sale — when price drops, you buy MORE. That's the whole law in one instinct. > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** Always mention **"Ceteris Paribus"** (other factors constant) when writing the Law of Demand definition — examiners specifically look for this phrase for full marks. It signals you understand that only price is changing, not income, tastes, or competitor prices. --- ### 📋 Demand Schedule A **table** showing the relationship between price and quantity demanded. | Price (₹) | Quantity Demanded | |---|---| | 10 | 8 | | 20 | 6 | | 30 | 4 | --- ### 📈 Demand Curve ⭐⭐⭐ A **graph** plotting the Demand Schedule. - **Y-axis** → Price - **X-axis** → Quantity Demanded - **Shape** → **Downward sloping** (because Price↑ causes Demand↓) ``` Price | |\ | \ | \ | \ | \ |_____\_________ Quantity ``` > 🧠 **PYQ Trick:** "Demand Curve → Always Downward." If a question shows an upward curve and calls it a demand curve, it's WRONG (unless it's the rare exception: Giffen/Veblen goods — not covered in this transcript, but good to know exists). --- ### 👤 vs 🌍 Individual Demand vs Market Demand ⭐⭐⭐ | | Individual Demand | Market Demand | |---|---|---| | **Meaning** | Demand of ONE buyer | Demand of ALL buyers combined | | **Formula** | — | Sum of all individual demands | | **Example** | Rahul buys 4 pens | Rahul (4) + Rohan (3) = **7 pens** | ``` Market Demand = Individual A + Individual B + Individual C + ... ``` > 💼 **Business Insight:** Companies almost never plan production around *one* customer's demand — they forecast **Market Demand** (aggregated across millions of buyers) to decide factory output, inventory, and pricing strategy. --- ## 3️⃣ SUPPLY ⭐⭐⭐ ### 📖 Definition (exam-safe) > Supply is the **quantity of a good or service that sellers are willing AND able to offer for sale at a given price, during a given time period.** ### 🔑 The Two-Condition Test (mirrors Demand, but from seller's side) | Condition | Meaning | |---|---| | **1. Willingness** | Seller wants to sell | | **2. Ability (Capacity)** | Seller can actually produce/supply enough | > 💼 **Business Scenario:** A factory has a production capacity of **10,000 units**. Can it "supply" 50,000 units even if willing? > → **No.** Ability (production capacity) is missing → Not valid Supply. ``` SUPPLY = WILLINGNESS TO SELL × PRODUCTION CAPACITY ``` --- ### 📈 Law of Supply ⭐⭐⭐ > **Definition:** *Ceteris Paribus*, **when price increases, quantity supplied increases** — and vice versa. ``` Price ↑ ──────────► Quantity Supplied ↑ Price ↓ ──────────► Quantity Supplied ↓ ``` **Why?** ``` Price rises → Higher profit margin → Sellers produce/sell more ``` > 💼 **Business Scenario:** Ice cream selling price rises in summer → Shopkeepers ramp up production to capture higher profit → Supply increases. --- ### 📋 Supply Schedule Table showing relationship between Price and Quantity Supplied (same structure as Demand Schedule, but seller-side data). ### 📈 Supply Curve ⭐⭐⭐ - **Y-axis** → Price - **X-axis** → Quantity Supplied - **Shape** → **Upward sloping** (because Price↑ causes Supply↑) ``` Price | | / | / | / | / | / |____/___________ Quantity ``` ### 👤 vs 🌍 Individual Supply vs Market Supply Exactly the same logic as demand: ``` Market Supply = Sum of all Individual Supplies ``` --- ## 🔥 4️⃣ MASTER COMPARISON: DEMAND vs SUPPLY (Highest PYQ Value) | Feature | 📉 Demand | 📈 Supply | |---|---|---| | **Represents** | Buyer's side | Seller's side | | **Relationship with Price** | Price ↑ → Demand ↓ (inverse) | Price ↑ → Supply ↑ (direct) | | **Curve shape** | Downward sloping | Upward sloping | | **Two conditions needed** | Willing + Able to **BUY** | Willing + Able to **SELL** | | **"Ability" depends on** | Purchasing power (money) | Production capacity | | **Aggregation rule** | Market Demand = Σ Individual Demands | Market Supply = Σ Individual Supplies | > 🧠 **Golden Memory Trick:** > **D**emand and **S**upply move in **opposite directions** when price changes — think of them as a **see-saw**: one side goes up, the other goes down. > ``` > Price ↑ > / \ > Demand ↓ Supply ↑ > ``` --- ## ⚡ 5️⃣ ONE-PAGE RAPID REVISION CHEAT SHEET ``` MARKET = Buyers + Sellers + Exchange PERFECT COMP. = Many sellers, no price control MONOPOLY = One seller, full price control OLIGOPOLY = Few sellers, joint influence DEMAND = Want + Money (both required) LAW OF DEMAND = Price↑ → Demand↓ (curve: ↘ downward) MARKET DEMAND = Sum of individual demands SUPPLY = Willingness + Production Capacity LAW OF SUPPLY = Price↑ → Supply↑ (curve: ↗ upward) MARKET SUPPLY = Sum of individual supplies RULE OF THUMB = Demand curve ↘ | Supply curve ↗ (opposite slopes — never mix them up!) ``` --- ##
