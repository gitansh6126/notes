# 📚 DEMAND & SUPPLY — Part 2: Shifts, Factors & Equilibrium ### *BDM | Economics for Managers | IITM Quiz + End-Term Ready* > 🔗 **Builds on Part 1:** In Part 1 you learned WHAT the demand/supply curves look like. Part 2 answers WHY they move — either sliding along the same curve, or jumping to a whole new curve. --- ## 🗺️ CHAPTER MIND MAP ``` WHY DO CURVES CHANGE? | +---------------+----------------+ | | PRICE changes NON-PRICE factors change | | MOVEMENT SHIFT (along the SAME curve) (to a NEW curve entirely) | | Up/Down the curve Right (increase) / Left (decrease) | +---------------------+----------------------+ | | DEMAND SHIFT FACTORS SUPPLY SHIFT FACTORS "I R T F B" "C T E S" Income, Related goods, Cost, Technology, Taste, Future exp., Buyers Expectations, Sellers | DEMAND CURVE + SUPPLY CURVE (plotted together) | Point where they CROSS | EQUILIBRIUM PRICE (Demand = Supply) | +---------------+----------------+ | | Price ABOVE equilibrium Price BELOW equilibrium | | SURPLUS SHORTAGE (Supply > Demand) (Demand > Supply) → Business cuts price → Business raises price ``` > 💼 **Business Insight:** This entire chapter is the theory behind **dynamic pricing**. Every time Uber shows "surge pricing," it's a live shortage (Demand > Supply) being corrected by raising price back toward equilibrium. --- ## 1️⃣ SHIFT vs MOVEMENT ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Most tested MCQ concept in this chapter) ### The One Rule That Decides Everything ``` ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ Did PRICE change? │ │ YES → MOVEMENT (same curve, new point) │ │ NO → SHIFT (entire curve relocates) │ └─────────────────────────────────────────────┘ ``` | | Movement | Shift | |---|---|---| | **Caused by** | Price change | Any factor *other than* price (income, taste, cost, tech...) | | **What happens to the curve** | Same curve — you slide along it | Whole new curve — it moves Right or Left | | **Direction described as** | Up / Down | Right (increase) / Left (decrease) | | **Example** | Govt raises tax → Price↑ → Demand↓ (slide down same curve) | Govt warns "smoking causes cancer" → Demand↓ at *every* price (curve shifts left) | ``` MOVEMENT (price changes) SHIFT (other factor changes) Price Price | \ | \ \ | \ ← slide along | \ \ ← whole new curve | \ same curve | \ \ |_______\___ Qty |_______\______\___ Qty D2(left) D1(right) ``` > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** **S = Shift = Something else** changes. **M = Movement = Money** (price) changes. > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** This is the #1 trap question. If a scenario mentions "tax," "price rise/fall," or "cost to the buyer" → it's **Movement**. If it mentions "income," "taste," "trend," "population," "weather," "expectation" → it's a **Shift**. Train yourself to scan for the word "price" first. > ❌ **Common Mistake:** Students think ANY change in demand = shift. Wrong — if the change was *triggered by price*, it's movement along the same curve, not a new curve. --- ## 2️⃣ FACTORS AFFECTING DEMAND (Shift Factors) ⭐⭐⭐ ### 🧠 Mnemonic: **I R T F B** ``` I → Income R → price of Related goods (substitutes/complements) T → Taste & preference F → Future price expectations B → number of Buyers ``` ### A. Income | Good Type | Income ↑ | Real Example | |---|---|---| | **Normal Goods** | Demand ↑ | Salary rises → buy more cars | | **Inferior Goods** | Demand ↓ | Salary rises → stop taking buses, buy a car instead | > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** *Normal* goods behave *normally* (both go up together). *Inferior* goods get dropped once you can afford better. --- ### B. Price of Related Goods ⭐⭐⭐ | Type | Relationship | If Price of Good A ↓ | Real Example | |---|---|---|---| | **Substitutes** | Replace each other | Demand for Good B **↓** | Tea ↔ Coffee, Ice Cream ↔ Frozen Yogurt | | **Complements** | Used together | Demand for Good B **↑** | Car + Petrol, Phone + Charger, Ice Cream + Hot Fudge | ``` SUBSTITUTES → move OPPOSITE directions (Ice cream ↓ price → Frozen Yogurt demand ↓) COMPLEMENTS → move SAME direction (Ice cream ↓ price → Hot Fudge demand ↑) ``` > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** **Substitute = Replace** (rivals). **Complementary = Together** (partners). > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** Examiners love flipping this — they'll say "price of tea falls, what happens to coffee demand?" Answer: Coffee demand **falls** too (people switch to now-cheaper tea) — NOT rises. Always trace the logic, don't guess by direction of the word "falls." --- ### C. Taste & Preference Consumers like a product more → Demand increases. *Example: Fitness trend rises → Protein shake demand increases.* ### D. Future Price Expectations Consumers expect prices to rise → they buy MORE **today**. *Example: Petrol price hike expected → people fill tanks today.* ### E. Number of Buyers More buyers in the market → Demand increases. *Example: A factory hires 2,000 new workers in a town → demand for housing, food, and clothing all rise.* --- ### 📊 Exam Summary Table — Demand Factors | Factor | Effect on Demand | |---|---| | Income ↑ (Normal Goods) | ↑ | | Income ↑ (Inferior Goods) | ↓ | | Price of Substitute ↓ | Demand for other good ↓ | | Price of Complement ↓ | Demand for other good ↑ | | Taste improves | ↑ | | More buyers enter market | ↑ | | Future price rise expected | ↑ (buy now) | --- ## 3️⃣ FACTORS AFFECTING SUPPLY (Shift Factors) ⭐⭐⭐ ### 🧠 Mnemonic: **C T E S** ``` C → Cost of inputs T → Technology E → Expectations (future price) S → number of Sellers ``` | Factor | Effect | Logic Chain | Example | |---|---|---|---| | **Input Cost ↑** | Supply ↓ | Cost↑ → Profit↓ → Less produced | Steel price rises → car supply falls | | **Technology improves** | Supply ↑ | Tech↑ → Production cost↓ → More produced | Automation/AI/robots in factories | | **Future price expected ↑** | *Current* Supply ↓ | Seller hoards stock to sell later at higher price | Farmer stores grain expecting price rise | | **More Sellers enter** | Supply ↑ | More firms = more combined output | New competitors enter the market | > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** Supply Shift = **C T E S** — say it like a name: "See-Tess." > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** Notice that "future expectations" pulls Demand **UP** (buy now) but pulls current Supply **DOWN** (sell later, hoard now). This asymmetry is a favorite trick question — don't assume expectations always mean "more." --- ## 🔥 4️⃣ MASTER COMPARISON: Demand Shift vs Supply Shift Factors | | Demand Shift (I R T F B) | Supply Shift (C T E S) | |---|---|---| | Income | ✔ Direct factor | ✘ Not applicable | | Related goods' price | ✔ Substitutes/Complements | ✘ Not applicable | | Taste/Preference | ✔ | ✘ | | Input Cost | ✘ | ✔ Cost↑ → Supply↓ | | Technology | ✘ | ✔ Tech↑ → Supply↑ | | Future Expectations | ✔ Buy more now | ✔ Sell less now (hoard) | | Number of participants | ✔ More buyers → Demand↑ | ✔ More sellers → Supply↑ | --- ## 5️⃣ EQUILIBRIUM PRICE ⭐⭐⭐⭐ ### 📖 Definition > The **equilibrium price** is the price at which **Quantity Demanded = Quantity Supplied**. At this point, the market "clears" — no leftover goods, no unmet demand. ``` Demand Curve ↘ \ \ ← EQUILIBRIUM POINT (D = S) / Supply Curve ↗ ``` ``` Price | Supply (↗) | / | / E → |------●------ ← Equilibrium Price | /| | / | | / | | Demand (↘) |________________ Quantity ↑ Equilibrium Quantity ``` > 🧠 **Memory Trick:** Equilibrium = **Balance**. Think of a see-saw perfectly level — Demand and Supply exactly matched. **Equilibrium Quantity** = the quantity bought and sold *at* the equilibrium price (where the two curves cross). --- ## 6️⃣ SURPLUS vs SHORTAGE ⭐⭐⭐⭐ (Very high PYQ value) | | 📦 Surplus | 🔥 Shortage | |---|---|---| | **Condition** | Market Price **>** Equilibrium Price | Market Price **<** Equilibrium Price | | **Supply vs Demand** | Supply > Demand | Demand > Supply | | **What happens** | Extra unsold goods pile up | Stock runs out; buyers can't get enough | | **Real Example** | Too many shoes made, not enough buyers | Low price → everyone wants it → stock finishes fast | | **Business Response** | 🔽 **Reduce price** (clear excess stock) | 🔼 **Increase price** (ration scarce supply) | | **Memory Trick** | Surplus = **Extra Supply** | Shortage = **Extra Demand** | ``` Price | Surplus |═══════● ← Price set ABOVE equilibrium (unsold stock) | | Equil. |-------● ← D = S (market clears) | | Shortage |═══════● ← Price set BELOW equilibrium (buyers can't get enough) |________________ Quantity ``` > 💼 **Business Scenario:** A retailer overprices new sneakers → shelves stay full (**Surplus**) → they run a "Flat 40% Off" sale to push price back down toward equilibrium. > A concert under-prices tickets → sells out in seconds, thousands miss out (**Shortage**) → next year, dynamic/surge pricing is introduced to capture true equilibrium price. > ⚠️ **Exam Tip:** A classic exam flip: *"Market price is above equilibrium — is this shortage or surplus?"* → Above equilibrium always means **too high a price** → buyers pull back, sellers pile up stock → **Surplus**. Anchor this to the price direction, not the word itself. --- ## 7️⃣ WORKED EXAMPLE — Seasonal Demand Shift (Summer Ice Cream) **Scenario:** Summer arrives → Ice cream demand curve shifts **right** (a shift, not movement — season is a taste/preference-driving factor, not a price change). | | Old (Winter) | New (Summer) | |---|---|---| | Price | ₹2.0 | ₹2.5 | | Quantity | 7 | 10 | **Result:** Because Demand shifted right while Supply stayed the same, the *new equilibrium* settles at a **higher price AND higher quantity**. ``` Price | D2 (summer, shifted right) | D1 \ / | (winter)\ / Supply 2.5 → |-----------● 2.0 → |--------● |________________ Quantity 7 10 ``` > 🧠 **Key takeaway:** A demand shift moves the *entire equilibrium point* — both price and quantity change together. This is different from a simple movement, which only shows a trade-off along one fixed curve. --- ## ⚡ 8️⃣ ONE-PAGE RAPID REVISION CHEAT SHEET ``` MOVEMENT = Price changes → same curve, new point SHIFT = Non-price factor changes → entirely new curve DEMAND SHIFT FACTORS (I R T F B) Income | Related goods' price | Taste | Future expectations | Buyers SUPPLY SHIFT FACTORS (C T E S) Cost | Technology | Expectations | Sellers NORMAL GOODS : Income↑ → Demand↑ INFERIOR GOODS : Income↑ → Demand↓ SUBSTITUTES : Price of A↓ → Demand for B↓ (rivals) COMPLEMENTS : Price of A↓ → Demand for B↑ (partners) INPUT COST↑ → Supply↓ TECHNOLOGY↑ → Supply↑ FUTURE PRICE↑ exp → Demand↑ (buy now) | Supply↓ (hoard now) EQUILIBRIUM = Demand = Supply (market clears) SURPLUS = Price > Equilibrium → Supply > Demand → REDUCE price SHORTAGE = Price < Equilibrium → Demand > Supply → INCREASE price ``` --- ## 🎯 9️⃣ EXPECTED PYQs — with 1-line answer keys | # | Question | Quick Answer Key | |---|---|---| | 1 | Define equilibrium price. | Price where Qty demanded = Qty supplied | | 2 | Explain equilibrium quantity. | Quantity transacted at the equilibrium price | | 3 | Difference: shift vs movement of demand curve. | Shift = non-price factor; Movement = price change | | 4 | Factors affecting demand. | Income, Related goods, Taste, Future exp., Buyers (I R T F B) | | 5 | Factors affecting supply. | Cost, Technology, Expectations, Sellers (C T E S) | | 6 | Substitute vs complementary goods, with examples. | Substitute = replace (tea/coffee); Complement = together (car/petrol) | | 7 | Explain surplus and shortage. | Surplus: Price>Equilibrium, unsold stock; Shortage: Price
